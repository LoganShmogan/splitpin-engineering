# Owner guide

For Jason Wilson, owner of Split Pin Engineering. This explains how your website works, what you
need to keep an eye on, and how to get things changed. You don't need to touch any code:
Savepoint looks after that.

## Your website

Address: **https://splitpin-engineering.vercel.app**

It has six pages: Home, Services, About, Our Work (your photo gallery), Contact and Legal.
It's built to load fast on phones, because that's how most people will find you, and it
shows a "Call" button at the bottom of the screen on mobile so people can ring you with
one tap.

## How customers get in touch

There are three ways, and all of them come straight to you:

1. **Phone or text** to 022 016 7223. Tapping the number on a phone dials it.
2. **Email** to splitpin176v@gmail.com. Tapping the address opens their email app.
3. **The quote form** on the Contact page. When someone fills it in and presses send, it
   opens *their* email app with a message already written to you, which they then send.
   The email arrives in your Gmail with a subject like
   `Quote request: Gates & fencing (Sam Smith)`.

The website itself doesn't store any messages. If something is sent, it's in your Gmail.

**Worth knowing:** because the form uses the customer's own email app, someone could fill
it in and then not press send in their email app. If you'd like form enquiries to arrive
no matter what, Savepoint can connect the form to a form service. Ask if you want this.

### Tips

- Check your Gmail spam folder now and then, especially for the first few weeks.
- Reply quickly. People asking for quotes often contact two or three businesses.
- If you change your phone number or email, tell Savepoint straight away. They appear on
  every page and in the information Google reads about your business.

## Keeping the site up to date

Send Savepoint a text or email when any of these change:

- your phone number, email, hours or the area you cover;
- the services you offer, or one you want to push harder;
- if you register for GST, or change the business name or structure (for example, if you
  form a company);
- if you start using a different way to take payment, or change your payment terms,
  deposits or quote validity.

### New photos for the gallery

Good photos are the most useful thing you can add. To send new ones:

1. Take the photo in good light, with the whole job in frame. Landscape works best.
2. Avoid number plates, house numbers and people's faces, or tell Savepoint so they can be
   cropped out.
3. Send the original photo (not a screenshot) with a short note: what it is and which
   category it fits (Gates & Fencing, Handrails, Trailers & Floats, Truck & Vehicle,
   Farm & Stockyards, Fabrication & Repair).

Savepoint will shrink the photo for the web, remove the hidden location data that phones
save in photos, and add it to the gallery.

## The Legal page and what it means for you

The Legal page sets out your privacy policy, your terms of trade and your warranty. It's
written in plain English, and customers can see it at any time. Because it's published,
**you're expected to work the way it says**. In practice that means:

### Quotes and payment

- Quotes are valid for **30 days** unless you write otherwise on the quote.
- Tell customers whether they're getting a **quote** (fixed price) or an **estimate** (best
  guess). Put it in writing, even a text.
- Quote household customers **including GST** (if you're GST registered).
- Invoices are due within **7 days** unless you agree otherwise.
- For a big or custom job you can ask for a **deposit**. Put the amount on the quote.
- Before doing **extra work** that wasn't in the quote, explain it and get the customer's
  OK (a text is fine), unless it's urgent for safety.
- If a customer **cancels**, they pay for work done and materials you can't return, and you
  refund the rest of their deposit.
- Customers should collect finished work within 14 days. You can charge storage if it sits
  more than 30 days after that.

### Residential jobs of $30,000 or more

If a job is residential building work (for example, structural handrails, balustrades or
gates and fencing that form part of a house build) and costs **$30,000 or more including
GST**, the Building Act requires a **written contract** and a **disclosure statement and
checklist** before work starts. Talk to Savepoint or a lawyer if you take on a job this size
and need templates.

### Warranty

You've promised to stand behind your work. If a customer reports a problem, look at it and
fix it where it's your workmanship. The page lists what isn't covered (misuse, overloading,
lack of maintenance, other people's repairs, customer-supplied parts, and so on).

### Privacy

- Only use customer details for their job. Don't add them to marketing without asking.
- Keep your phone, laptop and Gmail password-protected. Turn on two-step verification for
  Gmail.
- Keep invoices and business records for **seven years** (a tax requirement), then delete
  or shred them.
- If someone asks for a copy of the information you hold about them, or asks you to
  correct it, you have to respond within **20 working days**. Ask Savepoint if you're not
  sure what to send.
- If customer information is lost or stolen in a way that could seriously harm someone
  (for example, a phone with customer details is stolen and wasn't locked), you may have
  to report it to the Privacy Commissioner. Get advice quickly if this happens.

### Photos of jobs

Before photographing a job for the website, ask the customer if they mind. If anyone asks
for a photo to be taken down, tell Savepoint and it'll be removed.
